Why your business needs a Google Business Profile

When someone searches "salon near me" or "plumber in your city", Google shows a map with three businesses before any website. That map box is where local customers decide. If you're not in it, the decision happens without you.

A complete profile — correct hours, real photos, recent reviews, replies from the owner — is often worth more than a website for a local business, and it's free. The work is in setting it up correctly: right categories, right service areas, photos that look like your actual business.

Do this today: search your own business name on Google. If the listing is missing, unclaimed, or shows wrong hours, that's costing you customers this week.

How a website increases trust (even if nobody "browses" it)

Most small-business websites don't get much traffic — and still pay for themselves. That's because their job isn't traffic. It's verification. A customer hears about you, searches your name, and decides in ten seconds whether you look like a real, established business.

A one-page site with your services, prices, photos, location and a WhatsApp button passes that test. No site — or an expired, broken one — fails it silently. You never hear from the customers you lose this way.

Do this today: search your business name in an incognito window. What shows up is your first impression.

Five branding mistakes small businesses make

1. A different logo everywhere. One version on the signboard, another on WhatsApp, a third on the visiting card. Consistency is what makes a brand feel established.

2. Too many colors and fonts. Pick two colors and one font. Boring is better than messy.

3. Designing for yourself, not customers. The question isn't "do I like it?" but "does this make a stranger trust us?"

4. Low-resolution everything. A stretched, pixelated logo says "we don't sweat details" — the opposite of what you want a customer thinking.

5. Rebranding instead of finishing. Most businesses don't need a new brand. They need their current one applied properly everywhere.

The 10-point website checklist

Check your website against this list. Every "no" is a customer leak:

1. Loads in under 3 seconds on a phone. 2. Says what you do in the first screen. 3. Shows your prices or price ranges. 4. Has a phone number that's tappable. 5. Has WhatsApp or a short contact form. 6. Shows your real location and hours. 7. Has photos of your actual work, not stock images. 8. Works on mobile without pinching to zoom. 9. Has your reviews or testimonials visible. 10. Was updated in the last six months.

Score 8+: you're fine. 5–7: fix the gaps — each is a quick job. Under 5: your website is working against you.

The small business marketing checklist

In order of impact for a local business. Don't start step 4 before finishing step 1:

1. Google Business Profile — claimed, complete, photographed. 2. Reviews — a habit of asking happy customers, and replies to every review. 3. WhatsApp Business — catalogue, quick replies, away message. 4. A simple website — even one page. 5. Consistent profiles — same name, logo and number everywhere. 6. Then, and only then, ads — money spent on ads before the first five steps leaks into a bucket with holes.

The pattern: visibility first, trust second, spend last.

SEO basics — what actually matters for small businesses

Ignore anyone promising "#1 on Google guaranteed." Nobody can guarantee that. For a small business, SEO comes down to a few unglamorous fundamentals:

Say where you are and what you do in your page titles ("Plumber in Indiranagar, Bengaluru" beats "Welcome to our website"). Be fast on mobile — slow sites rank lower and lose visitors anyway. Match one page to one intent — a page per service beats one page listing everything. Keep your name, address and phone identical across Google, your site and directories. Collect reviews — they influence local rankings directly.

Timeline honesty: SEO takes months, not days. Anyone who says otherwise is selling something else.
